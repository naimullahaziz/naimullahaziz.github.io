The colour system

Four brand colours, ten neutrals

Deep Navy carries the structure. The three accents supply the energy, and they are deployed sparingly rather than spread evenly. The neutral scale is derived from Deep Navy rather than from pure grey, so borders, placeholders and disabled states share the same undertone as the brand instead of sitting apart from it.

Deep Navy #001741 Structure, body copy, dark fields
Electric Blue #007BFF Navigation, borders, links
Vibrant Amber #FFB800 Primary call to action only
Dynamic Red #FF2A00 Alerts and the logo mark

Neutral scale, derived from Deep Navy

0 to 100 for page and section backgrounds. 200 to 400 for borders, placeholders and helper text. 500 to 800 for secondary text, headings and footers.

The rules

Where most palettes quietly fail

A palette is easy. Making it survive contact with real interfaces is the hard part. The document answers that with two binding rules rather than with taste.

Contrast, measured rather than guessed

Start a Project 17.7:1 · pass Deep Navy with white type
Book a Free Call 10.3:1 · pass Amber with Deep Navy type
Book a Free Call 1.7:1 · fail The same amber with white type
See Our Work 4.0:1 · large text only Electric Blue with white type

The finding that drives the whole system: amber can still be the loudest call to action on a page, provided the label is set in Deep Navy rather than white. At 10.3:1 it outperforms the blue button. The colour was never the problem, pairing it with white type was.

Proportion, fixed at 60-30-10

60% Navy & white 30% Blue 10% Amber & red

Amber appears twice at most per screen, and one amber call to action is enough. Without a ceiling, accents stop being accents.

Applied

The proof is the page you are on

This portfolio runs on that palette, and rebuilding it against the rules is what showed the rules working. The first pass used the colours decoratively, giving every section its own accent so the page would not feel monotonous. Measured against the document, amber appeared twelve times on the home page and red thirteen, against a stated ceiling of two.

12 → 3
Amber elements on the home page
13 → 2
Red elements on the home page
10.3:1
Contrast on the primary call to action

The fix was to assign colour by function rather than by section. Blue took the working load: navigation, links, card rules, badges. Amber was reduced to one call to action per page, set in navy type exactly as the contrast table prescribes. Red lost its job entirely, because a portfolio has no error states, which is the only thing red is allowed to mean.
