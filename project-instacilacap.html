The situation

A year that was supposed to be a gap

I had finished my degree in Bandung and was preparing for IELTS with friends, aiming at a master's abroad. Then my father died and the family business collapsed with him. I went home to Cilacap to be with my mother, and to look for work there instead.

That year I helped run her catering business while I waited for a teaching post to open. I wanted to promote the catering on Instagram, which in Cilacap at the time almost nobody was using seriously.

So I built the audience first. An account about local tourism in Cilacap, a place I thought deserved to be seen, with the catering sitting quietly inside a following that had its own reason to exist. One thing carried the other.

How it was done

Four moves, no budget

All four answer the same problem: no money, no audience, and nobody who owed me a favour. Pick one to see how it worked.

  1. Move 01

    Prove it before asking

    An empty account. Nobody joins nothing.

    • I published twenty-five pieces about local places myself before inviting a single person.
    • Joining stopped being a favour and became an invitation. Asking people to fill an empty account is asking for a favour; asking them to join something already worth watching is not.
  2. Move 02

    Pay in hobby, not money

    No budget, and I was not going to pretend there was one.

    • I looked for people already out shooting at weekends, and invited them to do the same thing together.
    • The work was the reward, so nobody was waiting to be paid.
  3. Move 03

    Stop being the bottleneck

    Nine of us, and every decision still came back to me.

    • We split it into shooting, selecting and publishing, and each part got an owner.
    • This is where I stopped being the photographer and started running it, and gave up the part I actually enjoyed.
  4. Move 04

    Borrow someone else’s audience

    Growth still depended on me posting, which does not scale.

    • I ran giveaways with local promoters and opened trips anyone could join.
    • People arrived for something they wanted to attend, not because I asked. Growth stopped depending on me posting.

The commercial goal was met. My mother's catering got the promotion it needed and the orders came in. That was the actual brief, and the community was how I paid for it without money.

What it cost: a year of weekends, and giving up the part I actually enjoyed, which was being the one behind the camera. What it produced was an audience of ten thousand from nothing, around fifty people organised around a shared hobby, and a small family business that got busier.
